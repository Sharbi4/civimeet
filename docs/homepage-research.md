# Homepage research and simplification

Reviewed October 31, 2026. These are design observations and published industry research, not CiviMeet customer interviews.

## Sources and decisions

- [Circle](https://circle.so/): clear category promise, product visuals, and prominent next step. Lead with what CiviMeet does and a demo request; avoid unsupported customer claims.
- [Mighty Networks](https://www.mightynetworks.com/): outcome-led community positioning and visual product exploration. Give visitors a quick interactive example without requiring a long page.
- [Higher Logic Thrive](https://www.higherlogic.com/thrive/community/): member engagement and staff efficiency are central benefits. Explain local partners, planning, and invitations in concrete terms.
- [Community Roundtable research](https://communityroundtable.com/what-we-do/research/the-state-of-community-management/socmcaac/): limited attention and resources support focusing on reduced administration and useful engagement. This is broader industry evidence, not proof of CiviMeet results.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/): secondary detail belongs behind intentional disclosure. Use an optional demo dialog and three expandable answers, with direct product and pricing links.

## Implementation

Replace the long homepage with three compact sections: benefit and example, three outcomes, and expandable answers. Keep the existing navigation, demo-request form, pricing page, full product walkthrough, and footer. Do not auto-open popups. Keep pricing visible and describe connected social publishing as still in development.

Check desktop and mobile width, keyboard dialog dismissal, optional detail disclosure, existing homepage component tests, type checking, and production build before publishing.
